The Puzzle Competition Entrance

There is a puzzle competition with the following entry rules:

  • Only groups of 3 people can enter.
  • The sum of their ages, multiplied by 7, must be strictly smaller than 1000.

There are 3 friends willing to enter the competition. Unfortunately they don't know their ages or their birthdays. But they know the following information:

  • Alice is twice as old as Bob, plus 6 years
  • Bob is one third as old as Gabriel, plus 2 years
  • Gabriel's age is given by the first two prime numbers, squared, plus 3 years

With elementary arithmetic (addition, multiplication, and division), there is no need for a calculator.

Are they young enough?

Solution

The first two prime numbers are $2$ and $3$. Gabriel's age is therefore $(2 \cdot 3)^2 + 3 = 6^2 + 3 = 36 + 3 = 39$.

Bob's age is $\frac{39}{3} + 2 = 13 + 2 = 15$.

Alice's age is $2 \cdot 15 + 6 = 36$.

The condition becomes $(36 + 15 + 39) \cdot 7 = 90 \cdot 7 = 630 < 1000$.

That means they are allowed to enter the puzzle competition.
