Symmetry and Conservation in Hamiltonian Systems

Exploring how continuous symmetries in the Lagrangian formulation elegantly map to the geometry of phase space via the Poisson bracket.

In analytical mechanics, the transition from configuration space to phase space involves the Legendre transformation. Given a Lagrangian $L(q, \dot{q}, t)$, we define the conjugate momenta as:

$$p_i = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i}$$

The Hamiltonian is then constructed as the Legendre transform of the Lagrangian with respect to the generalized velocities:

$$H(q, p, t) = \sum_{i} p_i \dot{q}_i - L(q, \dot{q}, t)$$

The Poisson Bracket and Conservation

The true power of the Hamiltonian formalism reveals itself when considering the time evolution of an arbitrary observable $f(q, p, t)$. Using Hamilton's equations, $\dot{q}_i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}$ and $\dot{p}_i = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}$, the total time derivative of $f$ is given by:

$$\frac{df}{dt} = \{f, H\} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}$$

Where the Poisson bracket $\{f, g\}$ is defined as:

$$\{f, g\} = \sum_i \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial q_i} \frac{\partial g}{\partial p_i} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial p_i} \frac{\partial g}{\partial q_i} \right)$$

This immediately implies a profound consequence: if an observable does not depend explicitly on time ($\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = 0$) and it commutes with the Hamiltonian under the Poisson bracket ($\{f, H\} = 0$), then $\frac{df}{dt} = 0$, meaning $f$ is a conserved quantity. This is the Hamiltonian manifestation of Noether's Theorem.
